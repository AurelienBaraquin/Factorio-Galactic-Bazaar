# Decisions log

Running record of the choices that are too small for an ADR but worth remembering: what was
decided and why. Newest entries at the bottom. A choice that shapes the architecture gets an ADR
in `docs/adr/` and a one-line pointer here.

Format: `YYYY-MM-DD — what was decided — why (the trade-off)`.

Never rewrite history: if a choice is reversed, add a new line that says so.

2026-10-29 — Target Factorio 2.0; Space Age is an optional dependency, never required — reach both vanilla and Space Age players; every Space Age feature must degrade cleanly when the mod is absent.
2026-10-29 — Market counterparties are both abstract NPC planets and the real Space Age planets when present — keeps the economy rich in vanilla while using Space Age content when available.
2026-10-29 — Solo first, multiplayer-safe architecture — ship sooner; per-force state (ADR-0001) keeps the door open to multiplayer.
2026-10-29 — Code, commits and docs in English; GitHub issues and board in French; in-game locales en + fr — English is the norm of the Factorio modding ecosystem; the owner tracks work in French.
2026-10-29 — Work flows through issue → branch → pull request, merged by the owner — every change is traceable to an item of the GitHub Project.
2026-10-29 — Target Factorio 2.1 instead of 2.0 (reverses the first line above); Space Age stays optional — 2.1.24 is the current stable release and a mod declaring 2.0 is reported not to load on 2.1.
2026-10-29 — Repository tooling lives in `tools/`, never in `scripts/` — `scripts/` is the conventional folder for the mod's runtime code (adapters in `scripts/`, pure logic in `scripts/core/`), and `tools/` is never shipped in the mod zip.
2026-10-29 — luacheck is the static-analysis gate (local and CI); LuaLS/FMTK type checking stays an optional editor aid — luacheck's exit code is reliable and verified, LuaLS's CLI check reporting is not.
2026-10-29 — busted 2.3.0 on Lua 5.2 runs the unit tests of scripts/core/ (local and CI) — it matches Factorio's Lua version and needs no game.
2026-10-29 — In-game tests use FactorioTest (factorio-test-cli 3.8.0, npm devDependency) on the headless server — it supports 2.1 and save/reload checks; its mod comes from the portal, so local runs and CI need a Factorio account token (FACTORIO_USERNAME / FACTORIO_TOKEN).
2026-10-29 — Packaging and play-test deploys use tools/build.py (Python standard library) with .gitattributes export-ignore as the single list of non-shipped files — no extra dependency, and the zip and the Windows copy always contain the same files; deploy copies instead of linking because Windows cannot follow WSL symlinks and the game's mod updater can delete through links.
2026-10-29 — Pricing parameters start as constants (TIME_COST 0.1 credit/s, MINING_COST 1 credit/s, TILE_FLUID_COST 0.001, primary tile fluid: water) — iron ore = 1 credit anchors the scale; overrides come with #10.
2026-10-29 — Graphics in three phases: composites of base-game sprites and icons until gameplay is stable, a visual identity document before 1.0, then final assets rendered from procedural Blender models scripted by an agent (owner: all art is made by AI) — base sprites are free, legal to reference and always valid; procedural renders keep Factorio's projection and lighting consistent, which 2D image generators do not; Space Age graphics are never referenced because the expansion is optional.
2026-10-30 — Orbital capsule start values: 30 s delay, 10 stacks per capsule, one launch per hub every 10 s, 5 % freight fee, 20 capsules queued per hub — noticeable latency and a throughput (1 stack/s per hub) below a belt, so buying does not replace production; to tune with play-testing (ADR-0006).
2026-10-30 — Quality value factor starts at 3 per quality level (legendary ×243) — a legendary item built through quality modules and recycling costs a few hundred normal ones; to tune with play-testing (ADR-0008).
2026-10-30 — Power trading start values: settlement every 5 s, 10 MW then 40 MW per substation, export price floor 0.5 × base value, technologies at 150 red+green and 300 red+green+blue — a substation level matches a few steam engines, so trading power complements generation instead of replacing it; to tune with play-testing (ADR-0009).
2026-10-30 — Price signals refresh every 10 s per force before telecommunications research — fresh enough for circuit logic that reacts to market moves over minutes, and cheap; #25 will shorten it by research level (ADR-0010).
2026-10-30 — Telecommunications periods 30 s / 10 s / 2 s and costs 100 red+green, 200 red+green after advanced circuits — the first level is cheap enough to matter as soon as trading does, the second needs oil processing; to tune with play-testing (ADR-0012).
2026-10-30 — Spoilable goods sell at the market price times their freshness (1 − spoil_percent), the market moving by the units sold; the export floor applies to that scaled price — see ADR-0013.
2026-10-30 — Fluids travel in the orbital capsules with 500 units taking the room of one item stack (a stack of 10 barrels), are priced by name whatever their temperature, and land at their default temperature — keeps one delivery queue for all cargo; temperature-dependent prices only matter for steam, which has no base value (#63).
2026-10-30 — Tier start values: shuttle 20 s delay, one shipment of 40 stacks every 5 s, 3 % fee; teleporter 1 s delay, 20 stacks every second, 1 % fee, queue of 10 — each tier ships at least four times the stacks per minute of the one below while staying bounded (ADR-0014).
2026-10-30 — A cargo shuttle burns 100 MJ of chemical fuel per shipment (one rocket fuel), taken when the order is paid, and an order the bay's fuel cannot cover falls back to the capsule instead of being refused — fuel is the shuttle's running cost, and a short bay must not stop trading (#65, ADR-0014).
2026-10-30 — The teleporter draws a constant 20 MW and works while its buffer is at least half full; without power its queued shipments wait instead of landing — the issue asks for a constant energy cost, and a power failure must have a visible effect without destroying paid cargo (#66).
2026-10-30 — The catapult sells its whole 48-slot inventory per shot, at most every 2 s, for 50 MJ from a hidden capacitor charging at up to 30 MW under it — about 24 stacks a second against an export chest's few stacks every visit, paid for in energy; a hidden helper entity is the only way a chest can draw electricity (#67).
2026-10-30 — The space logistics branch is the cargo shuttle, then the catapult and the teleporter after it; technology names are kept (renaming would lose research progress in saves), and a test enforces vanilla packs, pack inheritance and reachable recipe ingredients for every technology of the mod (#68).
2026-10-30 — The brief's open question on a unified contract model is settled by ADR-0015 and moved to its scope, with the owner's agreement.
2026-10-31 — Transit from NPC planets (#84) rides the orbital capsule tier (schedule and queue bound shared with the hub) plus one minute per distance unit, and loses a share drawn in (0, 2 % × distance), capped at 25 %, from a per-force random state in storage (a tick-seeded draw let a player time a near-zero loss); no separate interplanetary tier, so concessions and foundries need no new building.
2026-10-31 — Outsourced production (#86) books a planet crafting slot like a mining concession: the planet buys the ingredients itself and the force pays for the products at 1.05 × their base value (the balanced market buy price without freight fee, above a concession's 1.0 because the player picks any valued product by the hundred) per period shipped; the issue allowed the force to provide or buy the ingredients, and buying keeps the force from shipping goods to planets, which no part of the mod does yet.
2026-10-31 — Real planets (#87) are derived, not listed: every planet but Nauvis brings the item resources its map generation places and one signature crafting category — the category most of its planet-only recipes (met there, not on Nauvis) belong to; keeping every such category gave noise (Vulcanus chemistry and cryogenics from one acid recipe), keeping one gives metallurgy, electromagnetics, organic and cryogenics. It works for other mods' planets too.
2026-10-31 — Loans (#96) are contracts with an advance repaid by a charge (ADR-0021): tiers 5 000 / 20 000 / 100 000, 12 installments over an hour, 10 % − 0.25 % × reputation, debt cap 50 000 × the contract scale; a default leaves the rest owed as arrears taken from the available funds (overdraft included, so they cannot be deferred), and a loan is repaid in full instead of abandoned — neither way out lets a force keep the principal.
2026-10-31 — Financial engineering research (#98): `gb-credit` (after contracts, 150 red+green) gates borrowing only — loans taken before it, as in 0.1.26 saves, are still repaid and their arrears collected — and `gb-overdraft-1` (200 red+green) / `gb-overdraft-2` (300 red+green+blue, after chemical science) raise the overdraft limit from 10 000 to 25 000 / 100 000; the base stays 10 000 without research so no existing save is pushed beyond its limit, and the level is recorded per force (`overdraft_level`) on research events, force resets and configuration change, since the pure account cannot read research.
2026-10-31 — The overdraft research levels (#98) follow reputation: limit = 10 000 + (level limit − 10 000) × min(1, contract scale), so half the extra from reputation −5 down and never more than the full extra; the base stays unscaled so no save loses limit — research must not buy a poor-reputation force more credit than its counterparties would give (ADR-0020 amendment). Levels are also recorded again before every interest charge, a self-heal for research changed without per-technology events.
2026-10-31 — After play-testing 0.1.28 the owner found every feature working but the mod far too opaque ("il faudrait avoir de l'affichage pour absolument tout"); a readability milestone (M7: central window, account statement, freight view, building status, alerts, explanations, market item detail) now comes before risks and balancing (now M8), since balancing needs visible mechanics. The brief's priorities and success criteria are updated with the owner's agreement.
2026-10-31 — Notifications (#107) go through one module (`notifications.notify`) with a pure throttle per force, kind and group in `storage.notifications`: offers once a minute per kind of contract, a contract due once (2 min before its deadline), deliveries one alert per hub per unloading pass and at most every 30 s, state changes and contract outcomes every time (bounded by the 8 contract rows); three per-user settings (contracts, deliveries, finance) hide both alert and chat line — what is told is decided per force so every peer agrees, settings only filter what each player sees. Alerts without a building of their own point at the player's nearest hub, else the character: `add_custom_alert` needs an entity, and the game keeps one custom alert per entity (a later one replaces it) and drops them after a short time (measured on 2.1.24), so the chat line is the lasting record and deliveries, frequent, have none.
2026-10-31 — After review of #112: throttled notifications carry their counts (`pending`) to the next one told for their group, so delivery and offer counts are true; rental offers are told once per force per offer pass with the number of machines; the insolvency and bankruptcy chat lines ignore the finance setting (it hides only their alerts), since missing them costs the force everything; a merged force's throttle log is folded into the destination's instead of dropped, so a contract due is not told twice; the throttle logs are created on first use too, as a save of the same mod version never runs `on_configuration_changed`.
2026-10-31 — Building status (#106) is shown through `LuaEntity.custom_status` on every building of the mod (diode and text, read on hover), plus a rendered icon only while a building is stopped (no hub of its force in range, or no power), visible to its force, with a short text in alt mode — the game's own status display needs no new interface, and icons limited to problems keep a large base readable.
2026-10-31 — Statuses are refreshed by one round-robin pass of their own (every 10 ticks, each building once per 5 s up to 600 buildings, at most 20 per pass), which lists the module registries at the start of each cycle, rather than inside each module's own periodic pass — those passes run at different rates and several buildings have none (import chests, docking bays, teleporters, contract chests); the trading passes only record what their last visit did (`held`, the last power settlement, an order combinator's last reading). A status is written only when it changed (a `custom_status` write measured about 5 µs).
2026-10-31 — Building status, after review (#106): the buildings are kept in one list updated on build and destroy events (no per-cycle listing), a pass refreshes at most 12 of them and stops after 2 costly reads (a hub's tier and bays' fuel, a force's contract needs and price period, each read once per cycle), which bounds every tick at about 0.2 ms with 1000 buildings; texts state facts without ages or countdowns so that a status is only rewritten when something changed; a rented rental assembler keeps the game's own status (its crafting diagnostics), its contract progress being in the contracts window; a building lacking power itself has its own alert ("No power"), apart from a module whose hub is unpowered.
2026-10-31 — Building status budget restated as measured (#106 re-review): at most 0.3 ms per pass every 10 ticks with 1000 buildings (worst observed 0.23 ms on a shared machine), instead of 0.2 ms scaled against another benchmark; MAX_VISITS goes from 12 to 8 (a pass of 12 reached 0.39 ms), so 1000 buildings refresh every 21 s instead of 14 s, and each kind reserves its costly reads so that a pass never makes more than 2.
2026-10-31 — Logistics tab (#109): it lists a hub's modules by walking the module registries (entity_status.modules_of) rather than adding a per-hub index to storage, and keeps that list in the tab's view for 5 s (the status cycle) or until another hub is selected, reading statuses live — the walk is the tab's dearest read (about 0.3 ms per 1000 modules); rows remember what they last showed and write a cell only when it changed, which brought a refresh with 20 hubs, a full queue and 1000 modules from about 1.3 ms to about 0.45 ms. Goods shipped from a planet record the planet and the share lost on their capsules (`planet`, `loss`), since neither can be recovered once the lease or contract is gone; older capsules fall back to the contract's counterparty.
2026-10-31 — Logistics tab after review (#116): the tab no longer walks every module registry; the building status passes record each hub's modules by unit number (`storage.entity_status.members`/`joining`, swapped each cycle like the module counts) and `entity_status.modules_of` reads only those of the hub, re-checking each, so a refresh that lists the modules typically costs 0.51–0.74 ms instead of 0.79–1.11 ms, about the window's 0.7 ms budget (garbage-collection outliers aside); a module re-attached to another hub shows within one status cycle (5 s). Selecting a hub accepts only one of the player's force.
2026-10-31 — The Statement tab (#108) totals categories from a per-force ledger (`state.ledger`: sums per category since records began and per minute of game time over the last hour), written by `account.apply` at each transaction, not by walking the journal at each refresh: walking 1000 entries with their classification measured 0.4 to 0.8 ms per refresh in game, over the window's 0.7 ms budget, and the journal merges recurring trades and drops old entries, so its totals could not be exact. Entries also record their category, so a filtered page reads one field per entry.
2026-10-31 — The journal size goes from 100 to 500 entries (#108): 100 entries held under a minute and a half of a base trading 40 goods automatically (measured with `account.apply` under Lua 5.2), too short to read; 500 hold about 7 minutes of it and much longer for a smaller base, at about 1 µs more per new entry and about 50 KB of save per force. 1000 was tried and dropped: a filtered page then cost 0.2 ms more per refresh in game, while totals no longer depend on the journal.
2026-10-31 — Explanations (#111): a tooltip whose text never changes is set once when its tab is built (column headers and cells, buttons, the Planets rows, the loan debt cap), and one that follows the force's state is written only when what it shows changed, keyed in the tab's view (Market: the score and "tier score"; Contracts: the score and each row's contract id and state) rather than rewritten on every refresh like the captions — the window refreshes once a second within 0.7 ms, and a cache of a few plain values keeps the cost flat (measured before and after: no change beyond run-to-run noise); rule figures (spread, freshness periods, fee and size factors, loan rate and cap, transit terms) are passed from the core constants, not written in the locale, so a tuning change cannot leave a tooltip wrong. The Tips and tricks texts and the building and research descriptions keep their figures in the locale, like the existing descriptions: they are static prototype text.
2026-10-31 — In-game help (#111) is a Tips and tricks category rather than Factoriopedia pages: one title entry suggested once Galactic Bazaar is researched, and one entry per system suggested once the research that brings it is done and the title has been read (the base game's pattern), so that a new player is not handed eighteen notifications at once; the quality entry exists only with the quality feature flag and the Space Age entry only with the space-age mod, so that neither describes rules the game does not have. Factoriopedia would have needed a prototype per system to hang the text on.
2026-10-31 — The locale is checked by a busted spec (`spec/locale_spec.lua`: no repeated key, same keys in English and French, every literal key the code names defined, every Tips and tricks entry named and described with and without Space Age and quality) instead of an in-game test: the headless server translates nothing (a `request_translations` test timed out, no client being connected), and reading the files catches a repeated key, which stops the mod from loading, before any game is started. Keys built at run time are not seen, so new ones are spelled out in the code.
2026-11-01 — Journal cost (#121): profiling `account.apply` (Lua 5.2 and in game, 200 recurring goods on a 500-entry journal) showed the cost was the search for the entry to merge into (kind, key and period compared from the end, about 200 entries deep), not only the `table.remove` shift a ring buffer would remove. Each force now keeps `journal_index` (`{journal, entries = {[kind "\n" key] = entry}}`, the last entry of each kind and key, the journal's own tables), maintained by `account.apply`, rebuilt from the journal when missing, made for another journal table or stale, and dropped on force merge, so no migration is needed and no reader of the journal changes. The merge rule is the old one exactly (the last open entry of the kind and key): when the indexed entry's period is over, the entries before it are searched as before, since after a merge of forces an earlier one may still be open; a first version picked the entry that started last, which review found changed the merge target after a force merge, and a differential spec against the old search, force merges included, now guards it. In game 28–30 µs → 15.6–18.5 µs per call. The journal stays a plain array, oldest first: a ring buffer would save about 6 µs more per call at 200 goods but change its shape for the Statement tab, force merges and the tests that read it. The index relies on a save keeping shared table references, which `tests/statement_gui.lua` checks across a reload; were it not kept, the index would be rebuilt once per load.
2026-11-01 — Logistics tab (#121): its pagers, counts, module summary, selected hub's header (the title keyed by hub and surface caption) and camera are written only when what they show changed (`written`); only the shown page of shipments is made into rows; the 5-s rescan checks the hub's modules against the listing (`entity_status.each_module_of`, no table per module) and lists and sorts again only when a module or diode changed, and a listing builds no intermediate table per module. Allocation per refresh: steady 50.7 → 30.6 KB, rescan 104.5 → 31.2 KB, listing 104.5 → 68.1 KB. The M7 full-suite outliers were garbage from placing 1000 buildings and from earlier test files collected during the refreshes measured, so the measuring test collects garbage before its averages; because that hides the collection a refresh's own garbage drives, it also logs allocation and single refreshes with a never-pausing collector. Budget statement (docs/window.md): met by the medians of steady and rescan refreshes and by every average, not by the worst cases (collection steps, up to 7.6 ms, mostly on listings, which happen on hub selection or module changes); reducing a listing further would mean not storing a row table per module.
2026-11-01 — Storage rental both ways (#119): NPC vaults filled through vault uplinks and shipped back to their hub, rental warehouses closed to the player and paid at maturity — see ADR-0024.
2026-11-01 — Storage rental budget (#119, after two reviews): vault uplinks are visited in a round robin like export chests, 2 every 6 ticks (20 a second), and a pass moves at most 10 stacks, so that its cost no longer grows with the number of uplinks or the goods in flight; an uplink is skipped before any read when no vault at its hub has a free or part-filled stack, its contents are read once, and its slots only for items a vault takes and has room for; whether an item may be stored and its stack size are cached at file scope by name (prototype-derived, the same on every peer, never in storage). Budget: 0.3 ms per pass (0.05 ms per tick on average). Measured with 18 uplinks of 20 storable stacks and 28 armours and 8 vaults, three runs: worst case a pass moving 10 stacks, 0.17–0.19 ms; full vaults 0.04–0.07 ms; vaults with room and only goods no vault takes 0.03 ms. Earlier versions: 50 ms (every stack against every vault, all uplinks each second), then 0.27–0.36 ms with full vaults, over the budget. Warehouse service pass: 0.008 ms with 9 rented warehouses, 0.055 ms when the 9 are collected at maturity.
2026-11-01 — Storage rental after review (#119): vault goods ship back item by item as far as the hub's capsule queue allows (no longer all or nothing), and ended vaults with goods left show on the Overview; Retrieve delivers only to the vault's own hub; capsules of vault goods return to the vault when their hub is removed; a vault is charged every period with or without a hub (skipping periods without one let a force mine its hub before each charge and keep the vault free); an active vault whose hub is gone takes over only a hub within 10 tiles of where it stood, otherwise stays unattached until its term ends; a deleted surface, told by index and name since indexes are reused, is the only case where vault goods go to another surface; warehouse rentals are custody contracts (no reputation on fulfilment, no due-soon alert) and their guarantee covers buying the goods back at acceptance with the price impact plus 10 % — see ADR-0024.
2026-11-01 — For the balancing milestone (#122): a warehouse rental's guarantee is fixed at acceptance, so a rise of the goods' buy price during the 30-minute term beyond about 20–30 % (market events of #120 can cause one) makes keeping the goods cheaper than buying them; options are a guarantee topped up during the term, a larger margin or shorter terms.
2026-11-01 — Market events (#120) are galaxy-wide and act as a shift of every force's displacement that only ever makes prices worse for the trader (a shortage raises the buy price, a surplus lowers the sell price, the other side staying plain), so no trade does better with an event than without it — see ADR-0025; this replaced, after three reviews of PR #125, a fixed course, an uncertain one and prices capped by the plain market's, which all left sure trades. The NPC drift of every market is salted each minute from the same entropy, so it cannot be computed from the key and force. Families for crises are item subgroups (`LuaPrototypeBase.subgroup`); the candidates are cached in `storage.market_events` and rebuilt with the base values (a draw that read every prototype measured 0.85 to 1.14 ms); events are indexed by key so that a market read without events of its key costs nothing measurable.
